INTRODUCTION

The Nila – Lifeline of Central Kerala

Bharathapuzha, popularly known as Nila, is one of the major west-flowing rivers of Kerala. It is the second-longest river in the state and an important river system in the southern part of India.

The river originates in the Anaimalai Hills of the Western Ghats and flows westward through parts of Tamil Nadu and Kerala before finally joining the Arabian Sea at Ponnani in Malappuram district.

The Bharathapuzha watershed is an important geographical unit that includes the main river, tributaries, streams, drainage channels, agricultural lands, settlements and different natural ecosystems.

The river plays an important role in agriculture, irrigation, biodiversity, settlement development and the cultural history of Kerala.

PHYSICAL GEOGRAPHY

Geographical Characteristics

The physical environment of the Bharathapuzha watershed reflects variations in relief, climate, geology, drainage and land use.

📍

Geographical Location

The watershed extends across parts of Kerala and Tamil Nadu. In Kerala, it covers the districts of Palakkad, Malappuram and Thrissur. The river flows westward towards the Arabian Sea.

⛰️

Topography and Relief

The basin includes highland, midland and lowland regions. The Western Ghats form the eastern highland region, while the western parts gradually descend towards the coastal plains.

🌧️

Climate and Rainfall

The watershed experiences tropical climatic conditions. The southwest monsoon contributes substantially to rainfall. Rainfall distribution varies between the Western Ghats, Palakkad region and coastal areas.

💧

Drainage Characteristics

The basin has a well-developed drainage network consisting of the main river, tributaries and smaller streams. Studies identify dendritic to sub-dendritic drainage patterns.

🪨

Geology and Soil

The watershed includes different geological formations such as crystalline rocks, laterite formations and recent riverine alluvium. Soil characteristics vary according to topography and parent material.

🌿

Natural Vegetation

The basin contains forest ecosystems, plantations, agricultural vegetation, riparian vegetation and other natural habitats. These support ecological functions and biodiversity.

DRAINAGE NETWORK

Major Tributaries of Bharathapuzha

The Bharathapuzha river system is formed by several tributaries originating from the Western Ghats and surrounding highland areas.

01. Chitturpuzha

An important tributary system draining parts of the Chittur region and surrounding areas.

02. Kalpathipuzha

A major tributary associated with the Palakkad region and its surrounding drainage network.

03. Gayathripuzha

A significant tributary flowing through parts of Palakkad and adjoining regions.

04. Thoothapuzha

An important tributary joining the Bharathapuzha system in the western part of the basin.

Drainage and River Course

The major tributaries collect water from different parts of the watershed and contribute to the main river system. The river flows towards the west and finally reaches the Arabian Sea at Ponnani. The drainage network is important for understanding runoff, flood behaviour, sediment transport and water availability.

LAND USE AND LAND COVER

Land Use Characteristics of the Basin

Land use and land cover influence the hydrological behaviour of a river basin. Forests, plantations, agricultural areas, settlements and water bodies affect infiltration, surface runoff and groundwater recharge.

A 2018 land-cover study of the Bharathapuzha basin identified plantations and forests as major land-cover categories, along with agriculture, built-up land, barren land and water bodies.

ENVIRONMENT AND SOCIETY

Importance of the Bharathapuzha Watershed

The river basin provides important ecological, economic, social and cultural benefits.

Agricultural Importance

The river and its tributaries support irrigation and agricultural activities in different parts of the basin.

Ecological Importance

Riverine habitats, wetlands and surrounding ecosystems support biodiversity and ecological processes.

Water Resources

The basin contributes to surface-water availability and groundwater recharge in the surrounding regions.

Cultural Heritage

Known as Nila, the river has a strong connection with Kerala's literature, traditions and cultural history.

Economic Importance

The watershed supports agriculture, fisheries, settlements and other livelihood activities.

Disaster Management

Watershed analysis helps understand flooding, runoff, drainage congestion, erosion and water-resource management.
